Walking counts more than you think. 15,000–20,000 steps a day keeps a real share of your aerobic base and keeps tendons used to load. This is very different from lying still for two weeks.

Your read on the Nimbus matched the data exactly: at 7:00/km ground contact dropped to 264 ms — that is the bounce you felt. At 8:00/km it sat at 275–280 ms, which is the heavy feeling. We do not yet know how it behaves on a long run, so test it in week 3 and compare.

Watch the ankle. The Nimbus has a taller stack, so there is more room for the ankle to roll. If that under-knee tightness comes back, switch to the Salomon straight away.

The one thing that matters

Cadence 178 or higher, stride under 70 cm. The ankle pain earlier this year came from overstriding — long steps landing ahead of your body. You fixed it, and the numbers prove it: stride went from 73 cm to 65 cm. When you get tired, stride stretches and cadence drops. That is the pattern to catch early.
